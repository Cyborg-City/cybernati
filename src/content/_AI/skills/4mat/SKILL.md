# 4MAT Framework Skill

## Overview

The 4MAT Framework is an instructional design model developed by Dr. Bernice McCarthy in the 1970s. It structures any explanation or lesson around four sequential questions that every learner asks:

| Quadrant | Question | Learner Type | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Engage** | **Why?** | Imaginative | Connect the topic to personal experience. Spark curiosity. |
| **Inform** | **What?** | Analytic | Deliver the core facts, concepts, and information. |
| **Practice** | **How?** | Common sense | Apply the knowledge through structured practice. |
| **Create** | **What If?** | Dynamic | Encourage creative, self-directed use of the knowledge. |

The cycle moves from right-brain (Why, What If) to left-brain (What, How) and back, creating a whole-brain learning experience.

---

## How to Use This Skill

### For Writing (Simplified Content)

When asked to simplify a dense topic or write a beginner-friendly version of existing content, structure the piece using the four quadrants in order:

1. **Why** — Open with a hook. A story, a surprising fact, a relatable question. Answer *"Why should I care about this?"* before teaching anything.
2. **What** — Present the essential facts. Keep it lean — just what the reader needs to understand the topic. No deep dives.
3. **How** — Walk through how it works. Concrete steps, real examples, the mechanism in action.
4. **What If** — End with the implications. *"What does this mean?"* or *"What if this changes how we think about X?"* Opens the door to further exploration.

**Writing tips for each quadrant:**
- **Why:** Short paragraphs. Personal. Conversational. No jargon.
- **What:** Clear headings. Bullet points for key facts. Define terms.
- **How:** Numbered steps. Examples. Case studies or walkthroughs.
- **What If:** Open-ended questions. Connections to the reader's life. A call to think further.

### For Teaching / Explaining Topics

When the user asks you to explain a topic (e.g., *"teach me about X"* or *"explain Y"*), follow the same cycle:

1. **Why** — Start with why the topic matters to the user specifically. Connect it to something they already know or care about.
2. **What** — Give them the core concepts. Define key terms. Use analogies.
3. **How** — Walk through an example together. Show the mechanism in action.
4. **What If** — Explore implications. Ask *"What would change if this were different?"* Encourage them to apply it.

## Example: Applying 4MAT to a Blog Post

**Original topic:** *Project PURSUE and 130 years of UAP narrative control*

| Quadrant | Simplified Version |
|---|---|
| **Why** | "The government just launched a website dumping thousands of secret UAP files. It sounds like full transparency. But there's a catch — and it's part of a pattern that's over a century old." |
| **What** | "Here's the quick history: mystery airships in the 1890s → Foo Fighters in WWII → flying saucers in 1947 → Roswell cover-up → Bob Lazar → PURSUE now." (1-2 sentences per beat) |
| **How** | "The government used four playbooks over 130 years: Deny. Debunk. Compartmentalize. Curate. Each one controlled the narrative in a different way." |
| **What If** | "What if PURSUE isn't the end of secrecy — just the most sophisticated form of it yet? The files are real. But who decides what gets released?" |
