## What Actually Happened

**June 1947:** Some pilot named Kenneth Arnold is flying near Mount Rainier and sees nine weird-looking craft zooming around. He describes them as moving "like a saucer skipping across water." The media hears "saucer" and loses their minds. The term "flying saucer" is born. And that's basically where the modern UFO thing starts.

**July 1947:** Something crashes on a ranch in Roswell, New Mexico. The local Air Force base puts out a press release saying they recovered a flying disc. Then hours later they're like "never mind, it's a weather balloon." Nobody buys it. Turns out decades later it was actually a top-secret spy balloon program called Project Mogul. They literally used the UFO hype as cover for Cold War espionage. It's like using a smoke machine to hide the fact that you're also on fire. Genius, honestly.

**1947–1969:** The Air Force keeps creating these study programs — Project Sign, Project Grudge, Project Blue Book. The formula is always the same: privately investigate, publicly tell everyone it's nothing. Swamp gas. Weather balloons. Mass hysteria. Move along.

**1989–2013:** A guy named Bob Lazar goes on TV and claims he worked at a secret base called S-4 reverse-engineering alien spacecraft. The government's response is basically "that place doesn't exist." Then in 2013, a FOIA request forces the CIA to admit Area 51 is real. So that whole "you're crazy, that's not a thing" stance? Yeah, it was a lie.

**May 8, 2026:** Project PURSUE goes live. The Department of War starts dumping declassified files in batches — FBI photos, Apollo mission transcripts, military sensor footage. All online. All free. After 130 years, they're finally letting people see what they've been sitting on.

## How the Playbook Works

If you look at the [[posts/from-classified-to-curated-project-pursue/index|last 130 years]], there's a pretty clear pattern. traces exactly how these moves evolved. The government has four moves, and they just keep cycling through them.

**Move 1: Deny.** Say nothing. Hope it goes away. This worked fine in the early days when most people didn't have radios, let alone the internet.

**Move 2: Debunk.** When people start asking questions, launch a study program. But the real job isn't finding answers — it's explaining things away. Swamp gas. Weather balloons. You know the drill.

**Move 3: Compartmentalize.** When the secret stuff gets too sensitive, hide behind the UFO rumors. The public is so focused on flying saucers that they don't notice the actual classified program underneath. It's a misdirection, and it worked for decades.

**Move 4: Curate.** This is where PURSUE comes in. Instead of denying or debunking, they just release the files. But here's the thing — they release them without explanation. "Here's the data. You figure it out." It's transparency without actually having to say anything.
